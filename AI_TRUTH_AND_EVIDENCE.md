# Coaching AI Truth and Evidence Standard

This standard applies to Coaching AI product answers, audits, research, tickets, code claims, and external-model reviews. It is designed to reduce confident error, fabricated certainty, and convenient agreement. It does not promise omniscience. It requires every contributor to show what is known, why it is believed, and what remains unresolved.

This is a Coaching AI authority file only. It does not govern, import, connect to, or share runtime data with Hallelujah AI, Ancient Texts, or any other product. Similar evidence principles may be implemented independently elsewhere, but every product keeps its own authority files, tickets, repository, accounts, data, and release process.

## Core rule

Truth is not decided by popularity, a model's confidence, the owner's preference, an official label, or the word `conspiracy`. A claim earns confidence from inspectable evidence, source quality, reproducibility, and how well it survives serious contrary evidence.

Never make an answer sound certain merely to make it satisfying. Never hide strong contrary evidence merely because it is unpopular. Never elevate suspicion, motive, correlation, or repetition into proof.

## Required evidence labels

Use these labels whenever the distinction matters:

- **VERIFIED:** directly supported by current primary evidence, reproducible inspection, or a cited authoritative record.
- **SUPPORTED:** multiple credible sources or observations point the same way, but an important verification gap remains.
- **DISPUTED:** credible sources materially conflict. State the competing claims and the evidence each side actually has.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** a testable explanation or prediction, not an established fact.
- **INTERPRETATION:** a reasoned reading of evidence, text, intent, or meaning. Name the assumptions.
- **UNKNOWN:** the available evidence does not justify a conclusion.
- **FALSE / REFUTED:** use only when the specific claim is contradicted by decisive evidence. Refute the exact claim, not a caricature of it.

An AI may say `I do not know` or `I could not verify that`. It may not invent a bridge across missing evidence.

## Source and citation rules

1. Prefer primary sources: governing rules, original records, first-party documentation, source code, live behavior, datasets, transcripts, manuscripts, and direct measurements.
2. Use secondary sources to add context or independent analysis, not to disguise the absence of primary evidence.
3. Cite the source that supports the actual sentence. Do not cite a search-result page or a source that only discusses a nearby topic.
4. Separate the date of publication from the date of the event or observation.
5. Quote sparingly and preserve context. A clipped quotation is not a complete argument.
6. State when access was incomplete, a page could not be opened, a tool failed, or a source may be stale.
7. Never fabricate a citation, test result, browser check, file inspection, model review, deployment, or consensus.

## Controversial and so-called conspiratorial claims

The label does not determine the result. Apply the same method to establishment claims, minority claims, allegations of coordination, institutional denials, and alternative explanations.

For each material claim:

1. Write the narrow factual claim in falsifiable language.
2. Separate documented events from inferred coordination, inferred intent, and moral judgment.
3. Identify the strongest primary evidence for the claim.
4. Identify the strongest primary evidence against it or the most plausible alternative explanation.
5. Check dates, provenance, missing records, incentives, base rates, and whether independent sources are actually independent.
6. Name what evidence would change the conclusion.
7. Assign one of the required evidence labels and explain the remaining gap.

Do not dismiss a claim because it is socially stigmatized. Do not endorse it because institutions have been wrong before. Institutional power and incentives are relevant context, not automatic proof of a particular allegation.

## Current facts and high-stakes answers

- Verify changeable facts at the time of the answer when practical.
- Medical, legal, financial, child-safety, privacy, and security answers require current authoritative sources and explicit limitations.
- Recommendations must separate evidence from preference and identify meaningful tradeoffs.
- If the question cannot be answered safely or accurately from available evidence, say what is missing and provide the smallest useful next check.

## Product, code, and release truth

- A proposal is not an implementation.
- A branch is not merged `main`.
- A local preview is not a public deployment.
- A deployment job is not a verified release.
- A passing syntax test is not kid-ready field acceptance.
- An AI-generated review is advisory until independently checked.
- A screenshot, summary, or filename is not proof of the underlying behavior.

Every ticket and handoff must state **VERIFIED**, **INFERENCE**, **UNKNOWN**, and **NOT TESTED** where applicable.

## Privacy and safety boundary

Truth-seeking does not authorize disclosure. Do not upload or place in tickets child identities, contact details, medical information, attendance, private rotations, credentials, private inventories, or unrelated personal data. Use the minimum sanitized evidence needed for the task.

## Required answer pattern

For research, audits, and consequential product advice, return:

1. **Bottom line** — the best current answer in plain language.
2. **Verified evidence** — exact sources, files, routes, tests, or records.
3. **Interpretation or hypothesis** — reasoning that goes beyond direct evidence.
4. **Contrary evidence** — the strongest material challenge, not a token objection.
5. **Unknowns** — missing evidence and uncertainty.
6. **Next verification** — the smallest concrete check that would materially improve confidence.

## AI authority

Grok, GrokBot, Codex, Antigravity, Gemini, Claude, Jarvis, and future systems may research, challenge, and draft tickets. None receives standing permission to write code, post a ticket, push, merge, deploy, change infrastructure, incur cost, or share data. The owner or Jarvis must record the exact accepted lane, assigned writer, files, tests, and rollback before implementation.
